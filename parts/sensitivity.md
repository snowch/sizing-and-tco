# Part VI — What the answer rests on

> Given a wide interval, what is the one thing to go and do about it?

An interval describes a problem without saying what to do about it. This part answers that, and
then shows the limit of everything the book has built.

**[ch18 · Which input to go and measure](#which-input-is-the-answer)** ranks the inputs by how far
each one moves the answer, and then answers the question a ranking raises: what measuring the top
one would buy, as an upper bound. Two of its results would carry the chapter on their own — the
book's most carefully measured constants buy nothing, and in the observability model the input
that ties for first cannot be measured at all.

**[ch19 · The missing node](#the-missing-node)** is the error no technique in this book can rank,
because it is not in the list. A model can be wrong about a number or wrong about its shape;
everything in Parts IV and VI is about the first, and the second is where the machinery stops
helping and starts flattering.

The order matters. A reader who does [ch18](#which-input-is-the-answer) without
[ch19](#the-missing-node) has learned to optimise a list without asking whether the list is
complete.
