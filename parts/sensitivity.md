# Part VI — Sensitivity

> Given a wide interval, what is the one thing to go and do about it?

An interval describes a problem and does not tell you what to do about it. This part tells you
what to do about it, and then shows you the limit of everything the book has built.

[ch19](#which-input-is-the-answer) ranks the inputs by how far each one moves the answer, and then
answers the question a ranking raises: what measuring the top one would buy, as an upper bound.
Two of its results would carry the chapter on their own. The book's most carefully measured
constants buy nothing. And in the observability model, the input that ties for first cannot be
measured at all.

[ch20](#the-missing-node) is the error no technique in this book can rank, because it is not in
the list. A model can be wrong about a number or wrong about its shape; everything in Parts IV and
VI is about the first, and the second is where the machinery stops helping and starts flattering.

The order matters. A reader who does [ch19](#which-input-is-the-answer) without
[ch20](#the-missing-node) has learned to optimise a list without asking whether the list is
complete.
