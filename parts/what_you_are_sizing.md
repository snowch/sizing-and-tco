# Part I — What you are sizing

> Which quantities size a system, where they come from, and how much any of them is worth?

The machinery can hold a quantity and check it. This part is about which quantities are worth
holding, and about the fact that a number written in a model file is a claim somebody is making.

[ch02](#what-a-workload-is) separates rates from levels, and what the world does to you from what
you have decided to do about it. The second split is the one a sizing conversation should be
about and usually is not.

[ch03](#where-the-numbers-come-from) is the difference between a number you measured, a number you
were told and a number you decided — which is invisible once all three are cells in one column.
It is also where a *measured constant* stops being a fact about the world and becomes a fact
about some software at some version.

[ch04](#peak-mean-and-growth) is the input that does most of the damage in this book, and the one
no amount of provenance discipline can turn into a measurement.

Nothing here is about what a system *does* with the demand. That is Part II, and it is where the
arithmetic stops being multiplication.
